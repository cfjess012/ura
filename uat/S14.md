### NFR-23 — Every authored condition renders as one English sentence in the instrument’s own words with no identifier in it, and every validator lints authored conditions at import for rules that cannot fire or cannot fail

- **What was done:** `src/lib/condition-text.ts` renders any condition as one sentence from a `Vocabulary` the caller supplies; `condition-vocabulary.ts` builds that vocabulary from the shipped instrument (risk-area names, intake labels, path labels, severity question names) and never falls back to an id — an unknown field is "a question". `src/lib/condition-lint.ts` refuses a rule that cannot fire (an option the field never offers, an unknown field, an empty operator, a `blank` where it is not allowed, the wrong operator shape for a single- or multi-answer field, a severity comparison on a non-severity) or cannot fail (equal and not-equal the same value, two disjoint equals on one field, a floor above a ceiling, blank-and-answered, `not answered`, an empty group, a rule written twice). `condition-known.ts` builds the lint's knowledge from plain data so the instrument validator (per edition, paths unseen at gate time), the Tier-3 validator (which had no field check at all — a typo was a follow-up that silently never appeared) and S24's authoring screen share one builder without a cycle. Both validators lint at import; an offending edition fails the build with the sentence.
- **What was observed:** Every gate rule, derived path, intake reveal and Tier-3 follow-up in the shipped instrument lints clean. Every rendered sentence for the shipped rules is free of identifiers (a regex over `gate.`/`sev.`/`path.`/`t3.`/`UPPER_CASE` ids). Each lint class produces its sentence on a fixture. `blank` is refused in routing and allowed in intake visibility.
- **Result:** pass
- **Evidence:** `test/unit/condition-text.test.ts` (8), `test/unit/condition-lint.test.ts` (14, including "the shipped instrument lints clean"); the validators' own tests unchanged.



## Findings

- **The Tier-3 validator had no field check at all.** A follow-up whose `when` named a misspelt field validated as well-formed and then never appeared — the silent no-op one tier down from the gate check that already existed. Closed: the lint runs at Tier-3 import with the shipped areas, intake fields and severity questions as its knowledge. Severity: medium (content, not a live defect — the three shipped follow-ups were correct).
- **Two validators probed a condition's shape by hand** (`"includesAny" in condition`, `rule.when.field`), which a nested rule would have broken silently. Both now walk `leavesOf`. `condition-lint.ts` reads a condition's operators structurally; `instrument.ts` still reads `includesAny` on the leaves it walks for the chained-derivation check, and nothing else does.
- **The renderer has no screen yet.** `describe()`/`shownBecause()` are exported and tested against every shipped rule, but every "Shown because…" on a screen still comes from an authored `because` string — deliberately, because the authored prose is better than the generated sentence where both exist. The renderer's first screen is S24's condition builder, where a person writes a rule and needs to read it back. Recorded as a deferral, not an oversight.
- **The verifier's first pass (FAIL) found the lint's list form filtering every empty-group message with its own** — an authored `{any: []}` on a derived path, or `{all: []}` on a pre-fill (which would pre-fill every project), passed both validators — and that `includes X` with `excludes X` on one field was never compared, though §19 names it. Both fixed, with the tautology (`X` or `not X`), the key-order duplicate, `equals` on the parts list, and a threshold on a non-numeric field (there is no numeric field yet; a `number` kind waits for the first) closed in the same pass; the intake sections gained a validator of their own at import.
- **`IntakeCondition` keeps its three operators.** Intake reveals route through `matches()` since S13 and could take the full language, but the intake sections are a TypeScript literal (plan S24 makes them data); widening the type before then invites rules nobody can author.

## Verifier

Independent slice-verifier, two passes, 2026-09-03.

**First pass: FAIL.** Two blocking findings in the lint: (1) `lintConditions` wrapped an authored list in a synthetic `all` and then filtered its own empty-group message — dropping every authored empty group with it, so `{any: []}` on a derived path (never lights) and `{all: []}` on a pre-fill (would pre-fill every project) passed both validators; (2) `includes X` with `excludes X` on one field, the case §19 names, was never compared. Eight more: the "byte for byte" claim was false (a single answer now satisfies includes/excludes as a list of one — §19's scalar case — and nothing said so); `equals` on the parts list and thresholds on a date linted clean and could never fire; `matches` threw on a prototype-named field; `X` beside `not X`, the `equals V or not-equals V` tautology and a key-reordered duplicate went unflagged; the renderer uttered an unknown part id and stuttered "Risk risk area"; the intake sections had no validator. The engine half held under attack — an empty form false for every negative operator and for `not`; the four original operators unchanged bar the one named case — and the renderer leaked no identifier across 146 shipped rules and 206 ids.

**Fixed:** the list form lints each member and then the cross-member checks with no filter; contradictions cover includes/excludes and, after the pass, every positive set wholly inside the negatives across operator shapes; tautologies and canonical duplicates are refused; `paths` refuses `equals`; a `number` kind exists and thresholds are refused on anything else; `answered()` accepts only a string or a list; the renderer says "a part nobody offers", "the AI & Model Risk area", "the answer to “…?” is …" and reads answered/blank about the question itself; `validateReveals` runs at intake import (exported and tested on a fixture); the record and G-77 name the one change.

**Second pass: PASS.** Every first-pass case re-attacked through the real validators on cloned `gates.json`/`tier3.json` and a mutated intake copy; the live walk on :3100 (a new assessment with AI, an outside company and Confidential data) showed the reveals, pre-fills and parts with their reasons and no identifier. Gates: typecheck clean, 894 unit, 56 integration, full E2E 74/74 on the tree. One narrow should-fix from the second pass — a contradiction across operator shapes (`equals Yes` beside `excludes Yes`) — and the prose notes were closed after the verdict, with tests, and the gate chain re-run.
