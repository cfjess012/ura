## The themes underneath the 11 items

Nine of the eleven are one idea: **the demo should behave like an enterprise
app that is wired to the systems a real one would be wired to.** Free-text
boxes become pickers backed by reference data — a people directory, a
business-unit list, a vendor list — with an escape hatch when the list is
wrong. Two items are different in kind and are called out below.

## The two that are not simply work

### #10 conflicts with G-39a, and the conflict is worth keeping

The ask — *default to the earlier answer, and tell them where it came from* —
is §24.6 ("nobody repeats themselves"), and the complaint is fair: the person
answered data classification at intake and is asked again in Tier 2.

But **G-39a settled that a derived value is offered, never pre-answered**, and
S4's done-when says the same: *"a derived band is offered, never
pre-answered."* The reason is not fussiness. A pre-selected answer that nobody
looked at is still recorded as that person's answer, attributed to them, and
attested later as if they had decided it. That is the failure this platform
exists to prevent.

**Proposed resolution, which gives the owner what they actually want without
reopening the decision:** keep the answer un-selected, and make the offer
carry its provenance properly — name the prior question, keep the note visible
*after* they answer rather than hiding it, and make accepting it one click.
Today the note says *"because you told us the most sensitive data involved is
Confidential"* and then disappears once answered; it never names the question,
and after the click there is nothing on screen saying where the value came
from.

If the owner still wants a true default after seeing that, it is a governance
change (Build Rule 10), not a wording tweak.

The second half of #10 — *"use reasoning to find other areas with this
problem"* — is a real audit and is tracked separately: **every Tier-2 question
whose answer is already implied by an intake or Tier-1 answer.**

### #1 introduces attachments, which is a slice, not a field

Upload is a new capability with three consequences the current build has none
of: somewhere to **store** bytes (§26.2 forbids the local filesystem, so
Postgres or S3), a **retention** answer (§3.4 is provisionally settled as
"synthetic pilot data only"), and a **classification** question, since the
whole point of this product is that documents carry sensitivity. It also
changes the AWS shape in `docs/migration-guide.md`.

Recommend: split #1. The **rewording** is a wording change and ships with the
reference-data work. The **upload** is its own slice with its own decisions.

## Answer to #9 — how do we know subcontractor reliance applies?

The owner is right that this does not align with the condition logic, and the
sharper version of the problem is that **the requester usually cannot know.**
A business owner buying a scheduling tool does not know who their vendor
subcontracts to. Asking them produces a guess, and a guess is exactly what
this instrument refuses everywhere else.

For one common case we do not have to ask, because the answer is structural:
**an AI product bought from a vendor always has a fourth party — the model
provider behind it.** The option's own helper text already says so. That makes
it a *derived* path, not a question: when `gate.ai = Yes` and
`gate.third-party = Yes`, `TPR_4P` should light itself with the reason
*"you told us this uses AI and involves a company outside ours, so the model
provider behind it is a fourth party"* — exactly the treatment `TPR_CONC` and
`PI_AI` already use.

The checkbox stays for the cases that are not derivable (a consultancy that
subcontracts, an outsourcer with a supply chain), where the requester
plausibly does know.

Net effect: the platform stops asking a question most people cannot answer,
and starts telling them something true they had not thought of — which is the
demo beat in miniature.
