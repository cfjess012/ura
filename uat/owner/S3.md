# Owner UAT script — S3 · Paths & engine

The second of the two rounds SPEC §10 requires. The builder round is
recorded in `uat/S3.md`; this is yours. Every check below has a **pass
line** that is objectively true or false on your screen.

**How to report:** "T4 failed" plus a screenshot is a complete bug report.
Don't diagnose it and don't propose a fix — that's my job, and a diagnosis
from the person testing is how a real defect gets talked out of existence.
Anything that isn't a false pass line isn't a defect: it's a candidate
requirement, and it parks until you decide it's worth a slice (Build Rules
7 and 9).

**Before you start.** App at http://localhost:3100. Top-right **Working as**
must read **Priya Sharma** (Requester) unless a check says otherwise. You'll
build two assessments:

- **Project A — "Cadenza scheduling"**: intake says it *uses AI*, *involves a
  company outside ours*, and handles personal information.
- **Project B — "Ledger tidy-up"**: intake says *No* to AI and *No* to third
  parties, and **"I'm not sure"** is used at least once.

Speed tags: **Fast** = instant. **Watch** = there is a visible wait to judge.

## Track 1 · The journey S3 built

### T1 · You can't narrow down what you haven't scoped · Fast
1. Start Project A and complete all four intake sections.
2. Before answering any risk area, go straight to
   `/projects/<A>/assess/paths` by typing the URL.

**Pass:** you do not see the narrowing screen. You land on an unanswered
risk area, with its question on screen.

### T2 · The narrowing screen arrives, and says what it's for · Fast
1. Answer every risk area. Say **Yes, it applies** to AI & Model Risk,
   Third-Party & Supply Chain, and Data Management & Privacy.

**Pass:** you land on a screen with the eyebrow **"Step 2 · Which parts
apply"**, the heading **"Narrow it down"**, and one card per area you said
Yes to — each with its own question, e.g. **"Which AI usage patterns
apply?"**.

### T3 · What you tick is counted back to you · Fast
1. Under AI, tick **"Outputs influence decisions about individuals"** and
   **"Generates content or communications"**.

**Pass:** the bar at the bottom reads **"2 selected"** (plus a clause about
items added for you, if any are showing), and a status region shows
**"Saving…"** then **"Saved"**. No second click is needed to make the tick
stick.

### T4 · The system adds a question and tells you why · Fast
1. Under **Data Management & Privacy**, tick **"Personal information is
   involved"**.

**Pass:** a panel appears tagged **"Added for you"** naming **"Personal
Information in AI / GenAI Use"**, with the reason: *"you told us personal
information is involved and that this uses AI — so we need to know how the
two meet"*. It appears without you reloading the page.

*This is the check that matters most to the demo. If it needs a reload,
that's a fail — say so.*

### T5 · Change your mind and the addition goes away · Fast
1. Untick **"Personal information is involved"**.

**Pass:** the "Added for you" panel for *Personal Information in AI / GenAI
Use* disappears. Nothing you ticked elsewhere changes. Re-tick it and it
comes back.

### T6 · Nothing selected is a real answer, not a blank · Fast
1. Untick everything in one area.

**Pass:** the bar reads **"Nothing selected yet — that's a valid answer if
none of them apply."** The forward button stays available; nothing turns
red and nothing nags you.

### T7 · Leaving by the sidebar doesn't lose your ticks · Fast
1. Tick two options. Immediately click a risk area in the left **Risk
   areas** rail.
2. Come back to the narrowing screen (rail, or browser Back).

**Pass:** both ticks are still there.

### T8 · You are never asked to become sure · Fast
1. In **Project B**, answer **"I'm not sure"** to the intake question
   *"Does this use AI or machine learning?"*, then complete the risk areas.

**Pass:** on the narrowing screen, the AI card carries a note reading
**"Leave this one to us"** and the sentence *"You told us you weren't sure
about this area, and we haven't asked you to become sure. Tick anything you
do know — a reviewer works out the rest."* You are **not** asked five
sharper AI questions as if the uncertainty had resolved itself.

### T9 · When there's nothing to narrow, there's still a way forward · Fast
1. In Project B, say **No, it doesn't** to every risk area.

**Pass:** the screen reads **"Nothing to narrow down"** and offers a button
that moves you on. There is no dead end where the only way forward is to
type a URL.

### T10 · Come back tomorrow and it's all still there · Fast
1. In Project A, reload the narrowing screen (⌘R).

**Pass:** every tick is exactly as you left it, and every "Added for you"
panel is still showing with the same reason.

## Track 2 · The unhappy paths

### T11 · A failure says what happened, in English · Watch
1. On the narrowing screen, turn off Wi-Fi (or DevTools → Network →
   Offline).
2. Tick an option.

**Pass:** a message appears in plain English — *"The server couldn't be
reached, so nothing was saved. Your selections are still on screen — try
again in a moment."* — followed by **"Reference"** and a short code. Your
ticks are still on screen. No stack trace, no error class name, no
`undefined`. Turn Wi-Fi back on, tick again, and it saves.

### T12 · Someone else's assessment isn't yours · Fast
1. Note Project A's URL. Switch **Working as** to **Noah Kahan**
   (Assessor), then to **Tom Holland** (Admin), pasting that URL each time.
2. Now switch to **Tom Holland** (Admin), start a third assessment, copy
   its URL, switch back to **Priya Sharma**, and paste it.

**Pass:** Priya is refused with a plain explanation, not a crash and not a
blank page. (Assessors and admins seeing it is correct — that's their job.)

### T13 · The forward control never swallows a click · Fast
1. Tick an option and, without pausing, click **"Next: how severe →"**.

**Pass:** it responds to that first press — you move on, or you see
"Saving…" and then move on. It does not sit there dead, and you never have
to press it twice.

## The one thing that isn't a T#

Build Rule 16 says critique is owed in both directions, and this is a
judgment only you can make:

**"Added for you" (T4) — relief or overreach?** It's the one moment the
system answers on the business user's behalf. Does the tag plus the reason
make you feel the work got smaller, or does it feel like something decided
for you? A gut reaction in one sentence is worth more than a considered
one. Either answer is cheap: "leave it" or "change six words".
