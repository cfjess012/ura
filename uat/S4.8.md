## Findings

**One defect found by walking, not by testing.** The Yes option's sub-label
read *"We'll ask more about this area later"* on all eleven areas — false for
the seven that ask nothing. Fixed by conditioning the copy and pinned.

**One defect the walk ALSO missed, found by the independent verifier (F10).**
Answering Yes navigated away before the declaration could be read, so the
slice whose entire purpose is saying where the product stops was itself
silent in the forward journey. My walk loaded the screen with the answer
already recorded and saw the block — a state a person reaches only by going
back. Fixed at 80776ff and re-measured by polling the DOM across the whole
interaction rather than sampling after it.

The lesson is narrower than "walk it": **checking a screen in the state you
put it in is not the same as walking the journey a person walks.** Both of
this slice's real defects were invisible to tests, and one was invisible to
a walk that skipped the transition.

Verifier F11 remains open (below).

## Agentic opportunity

An agent could tell a requester what a quiet area's Yes actually triggers
downstream — which reviewer group picks it up, what they typically ask for —
so "recorded for a reviewer" names a person and a next step rather than a
destination. Guardrails: it reads the routing already computed and the
reviewer groups already seeded; it never invents a service-level promise or
a date. Human decision it must never take: whether the area applies.
